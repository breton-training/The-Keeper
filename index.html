Chapter One

The Garden Assignment

The second chapter of Genesis is describing a completion. God has made the world, formed the man, planted the garden, sent the rivers out from a single source in four directions, and He placed the man in the garden deliberately — the Hebrew verb is the kind you use for setting something down where it belongs. Adam has been given work to do and a boundary to honor. He has been given a helper who is drawn from his own side. He walks in the cool of the day with the God who made him. Everything that was supposed to be there is there. The arrangement is whole. This book begins where that wholeness begins, because every page after it is in some sense about what was lost when the arrangement was broken, and what it cost to put it back.

Adam's placing was deliberate, so much so that the verse describing it uses two different words to say so. The Hebrew is short — about a dozen words — and the part that matters is two verbs. The Lord God took the man, and put him in the garden of Eden. The first verb is wayiqach (took). It is the verb you use when a person does something on purpose. The second verb is yanach (put). It is the verb you use when you set something down where it belongs. Not dropped. Not left. Stationed. Both verbs are active, and both verbs are God's. The man did not arrive in the garden by accident. He was taken from somewhere, he was placed somewhere, and the somewhere he was placed is named.

SourcesGenesis 2:4–25; Genesis 2:15; Genesis 3:8. Hebrew wayiqach, Strong's H3947; Hebrew yanach, Strong's H3240.
